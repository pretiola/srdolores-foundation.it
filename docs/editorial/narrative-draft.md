# Canonical narrative — working draft for factual review

Status: draft, not approved for publication. Prepared 19 September 2026.

## Usable factual core

The Sr. Dolores Foundation's website describes practical support for families and children in Binzi, Uganda. The current editorial priority is the Piglet Project's target of 60 families: five piglets per family, or 300 in total. The intended purpose is to help families develop more secure livelihoods through livestock and associated care and training. These are targets and intended benefits; a current count of deliveries and documented results is still needed.

The existing operating description says participating families prepare housing for piglets, with feeding equipment, training and supervision envisaged as part of the support. Before describing this as delivered support, confirm who provides each input, what households contribute, what the budget covers and what has already happened. The current site also describes education support, agriculture, water and housing work. Each activity needs a dated status and evidence before new outcome claims are made.

Fr. Emmanuel Kasibante is the contact named on the website. Its existing first-person account says Sister Dolores helped him become a Catholic priest and inspired a spirit of service. The next version of this story should use his own approved account, with enough geography and chronology to explain the connection accurately. Do not imply she worked in Binzi without evidence.

Pretiola's role in the supplied briefing is technical and strategic support to locally led work. The final public narrative should make clear who operates and decides priorities locally, who provides technical support, and who is responsible for receiving and reporting contributions.

## Questions needed to complete the two-page approved narrative

1. How would Fr. Emmanuel describe his relationship to Binzi, his present ministry and his return to grassroots work? Which dates and places may be published?
2. How did he first meet Sister Dolores? What specific assistance or influence did she provide? Which details are his recollection and which have documentary support?
3. Which congregation name, birthplace, dates, assignments and biographical events are confirmed? Who can verify these through family/congregation sources?
4. When did the current initiative begin, and how should its name and organizational status be described?
5. Why were livestock, agriculture, education, water and household support chosen locally?
6. How are participating families identified, and what do they contribute? Avoid exposing individual household vulnerabilities without permission.
7. What has actually been delivered, when, to how many households? What is still a target?
8. What has been difficult or unsuccessful? What changed as a result?
9. What is the next concrete locally selected milestone, cost and intended time period?
10. Who can approve public claims and what is the realistic reporting process?
