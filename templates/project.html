The Piglet Project in Binzi

Helping families work towards more secure livelihoods through livestock, practical skills and shared responsibility.

Our goal: 60 families

The project aims to support 60 families in Binzi, Uganda with five piglets each. That is a target of 300 piglets, alongside planned start-up feed, care and training.

These figures describe the project goal. They are not a count of families already supported or piglets already distributed.

Why this work matters

Families need reliable ways to meet everyday needs, including food, school materials, clothing and healthcare. The Piglet Project is intended to help households develop a source of income and strengthen their farming skills.

The families described in the project include widows, single-parent households and households where an older child has taken on a parent's responsibilities. Read about the intended beneficiaries.

How the project is intended to work

  1. Prepare together. Participating families prepare a place to house the piglets and take responsibility for their daily care.
  2. Provide the starting support. The plan includes piglets, start-up feed, feeding and drinking equipment, and veterinary supplies.
  3. Build practical skills. Training and supervision are intended to help families care for the animals. The original plan describes home visits over two months; current arrangements still need confirmation.
  4. Work towards lasting benefits. The intended outcomes are stronger household livelihoods and useful farming experience. Actual results depend on care, costs and local conditions, and need to be documented over time.

What a contribution can help provide
